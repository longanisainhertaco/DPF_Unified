# Dense Plasma Focus Discharge — Paper-Verified Narrative

*Every claim cited from PDFs read in session 2026-04-02.*

---

A dense plasma focus discharge begins when a capacitor bank — storing hundreds of kilojoules to megajoules of energy — is switched across a pair of coaxial electrodes separated by a ceramic insulator, inside a vacuum chamber filled with a few Torr of deuterium gas. The applied voltage drives a surface flashover across the insulator, ionizing a thin layer of gas and forming a conducting current sheath (Goyon et al. 2025, p.3: "the high voltage applied to the electrodes generates field-emitted electrons from the cathode that ionize the gas en route to the anode. This forms a conducting channel that completes the circuit"). The current flowing through this sheath generates an azimuthal (toroidal) magnetic field B_theta = mu_0 I / (2 pi r) (Fundamentals of Plasma Physics, Ch.13, p.341, Eq.2.4), and the resulting J x B Lorentz force accelerates the sheath axially away from the insulator in what Lee (2014, p.321) describes as a "snowplow model with an equation of motion which is coupled to a circuit equation." On large machines like MJOLNIR at Lawrence Livermore, the sheath runs down a 228.6 mm diameter copper anode surrounded by 24 cathode rods (Goyon 2025, p.2), reaching the anode tip in a few microseconds while the circuit current climbs toward megampere levels — MJOLNIR has been commissioned to 3.8 MA (Goyon 2025, p.1). On the 100 kV FAETON-I device at Fuse Energy, "the discharge generates approximately 1 MA of electrical current with a rise time of approximately 3.7 microseconds" (Damideh et al. 2025, p.2).

When the current sheath reaches the end of the anode, "the J x B force gains a radial component directed inward" (Goyon 2025, p.3) and the plasma begins converging toward the cylindrical axis — the radial implosion phase. The Lee model describes this stage with four coupled equations governing the inward shock speed, the axial column elongation, the magnetic piston velocity, and the circuit current (Lee 2014, p.321). The imploding plasma acts as a magnetic piston driving a shock wave ahead of it; the implosion velocity can be estimated as v_imp approximately equal to 950 km/s times I_imp divided by (R_imp times the square root of P_fill) (Goyon 2025, p.4, Eq.1). For MJOLNIR at 2 MA with R_imp = 2.5 cm, this gives approximately 259 km/s, confirmed by fast framing camera measurements at 279 +/- 28 km/s (Goyon 2025, p.4). As the shock front converges on the axis, the plasma compresses to extreme densities and temperatures. The shock stagnates, and "because the focus plasma is collisional, a reflected shock develops which moves radially outwards, whilst the radial current sheath continues to move inwards" (Lee 2014, p.321), nearly doubling the temperature. The result is a thin, elongated plasma column confined by its own magnetic field — the z-pinch. The equilibrium condition is a radial pressure balance: dp/dr = -J_z(r) B_theta(r) (Fundamentals of Plasma Physics, Ch.13, p.340, Eq.2.1), where the plasma's kinetic pressure is contained by the inward-directed magnetic force. The Bennett relation, I_0^2 = (8 pi / mu_0) k (T_e + T_i) N_l (Ch.13, p.342, Eq.2.15), gives the current required to confine a given line density of particles at a given temperature — for fusion-relevant conditions, this is on the order of a million amperes. The average stagnation temperature scales as T_st ~ 21 keV * I_imp^2 / (R_imp^2 * P_fill) (Goyon 2025, p.4, Eq.2).

The pinch is violently unstable. Magnetohydrodynamic instabilities — particularly the m=0 sausage mode — "cause the plasma column to break apart in a manner analogous to a classic m=0 instability" (Goyon 2025, p.4), producing "discrete transitions in the plasma column's behavior starting with an inward imploding sheath and ending in a broken apart central column" (Goyon 2025, p.3). This disruption generates electric fields on the order of MV/mm that accelerate ions to supra-thermal energies: "the electrons propagate primarily toward the anode where they generate x-ray radiation via bremsstrahlung... ions are accelerated toward the plasma assembled downstream of the pinch region and generate neutrons via beam-target interaction" (Goyon 2025, p.4). Multiple neutron generation events occur during a single discharge — thermonuclear fusion at stagnation, followed by beam-target events during each successive pinch disruption (Goyon 2025, Fig.4: three distinct neutron peaks at 0, ~5, and ~25 ns relative to stagnation). On FAETON-I, "the first dynamics-induced pulse of fast ion beams is produced just before stagnation, while the second pulse is generated toward the end of the pinch phase, merging from a multitude of instability-produced micro ion beams" (Damideh 2025, p.1), with peak yields of 8 x 10^10 neutrons per shot at 12 Torr (Damideh 2025, p.5). The entire sequence — from insulator flashover to the final neutron burst — unfolds in less than 10 microseconds, compressing megajoules of stored electrical energy into a pinch column millimeters in radius where, for a few tens of nanoseconds, matter exists at fusion-relevant conditions.
