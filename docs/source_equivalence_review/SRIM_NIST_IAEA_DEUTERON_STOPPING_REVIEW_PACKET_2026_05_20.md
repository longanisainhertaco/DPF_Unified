## 3. Source-equivalence argument (the case FOR)

- **Shared theoretical framework.** ICRU 49, SRIM-2013, NIST PSTAR, and the
  IAEA calculator all implement the Bethe–Bloch stopping formula
  `−dE/dx = (4πe⁴ z² N Z) / (m_e v²) · [ln(2 m_e v² / I) − ln(1−β²) − β²]`
  with I-value (mean excitation energy) tabulated or computed per material.
  ICRU 49 and SRIM-2013 both use the same functional form with different
  parametrizations of the I-value and shell corrections (Ziegler 2010 vs
  ICRU 49 Table A.4), but the differences in the cold-matter, intermediate-
  energy (0.1–10 MeV/u) regime are typically < 2–5% for light targets.
- **ICRU 49 and SRIM are cross-validated in the literature.** Multiple
  papers in the DPF neutron domain use SRIM as a surrogate for ICRU 49
  stopping data and cite both in the same sentence (e.g., Scholz et al.,
  Klír et al. neutron-yield papers). The KR file
  `accelerationof-protonsand-deuterons-upto35mevand-generationof1-3f0ebd08.md`
  confirms that thick-target yield calculations in z-pinch contexts cite
  both SRIM and ENDF as equivalent stopping references.
- **NIST PSTAR is the open-reference standard.** NIST PSTAR is the numerical
  implementation underlying both ICRU 37 (electron/positron) and the proton
  stopping supplement to ICRU 49 by the same NIST team (Berger, Coursey,
  Zucker). The PSTAR tables and ICRU 49 Table A.4 should be numerically
  consistent within < 1% for D₂ gas in the DPF beam-energy range (0.1–10
  MeV deuterons).
- **D₂ gas stopping is a well-determined quantity.** At the DPF beam-energy
  range, cold-gas stopping for deuterons on D₂ target is a standard
  nuclear-data problem; ICRU 49, SRIM, and PSTAR should all agree to within
  their respective uncertainty bands (5–8% per ICRU 49 §1.3).

## 4. Source-difference argument (the case AGAINST)

- **Dense-plasma stopping vs cold-matter stopping: the fundamental scope
  mismatch.** ICRU 49, SRIM, NIST PSTAR, and the IAEA calculator all compute
  stopping power in **cold, neutral matter** using bulk density and mean
  excitation energy of a neutral target. In the DPF pinch plasma (n_e ~
  10²³–10²⁴ m⁻³, T_e ~ 1–2 keV), the target is partially or fully ionized.
  Dense-plasma corrections to Bethe–Bloch stopping include:
  (a) Free-electron contribution (plasma collective excitations replace the
  bound-electron oscillators): the plasma frequency ω_p replaces the
  low-frequency cutoff, reducing the Coulomb logarithm.
  (b) Charge-state equilibration of the deuteron beam in the plasma differs
  from cold-gas charge-exchange.
  (c) Shell corrections and Barkas corrections change in partially-ionized
  targets.
  None of SRIM, NIST PSTAR, or the IAEA calculator model these effects.
  ICRU 49 includes a limited discussion (§3.5) of dense-matter corrections
  but the tabulated values are for cold material at bulk density.
- **SRIM parametrization of I-values differs from ICRU 49.** Ziegler (2010)
  uses a slightly different parametrization of the mean excitation energy I
  (Ziegler universal fit vs ICRU 49 per-element table). For hydrogen/deuterium
  targets (I_H = 19.2 eV, ICRU 49 §A.4; SRIM uses a very similar value but
  with a ZBL-potential shell-correction adjustment). In the overlap range the
  discrepancy is typically < 2%, but this has not been confirmed for D₂ gas
  in the DPF beam-energy range as part of this sprint.
- **SRIM is a binary, non-auditable tool.** SRIM source code is not publicly
  available. The internal numerical method (TRIM Monte Carlo + analytic
  Bethe–Bloch backbone) cannot be independently reviewed. Any systematic error
  in the SRIM parametrization for light targets (Z=1) would be undetectable
  without comparison against an independent source (e.g., NIST PSTAR or
  ICRU 49).
- **Density-effect correction at DPF-relevant pressures.** ICRU 49 includes
  the Sternheimer density-effect correction for condensed matter; for gas
  targets at fill pressures (3.5 Torr), the correction is negligible but must
  be confirmed explicitly. SRIM applies a simplified density correction; NIST
  PSTAR allows the user to set gas pressure for the density-effect correction.
  If the correction is applied inconsistently across tools, the tables would
  not be directly comparable.
- **Scope of NEUTRON-BLK-002 is beam-target yield, not transport closure.**
  The stopping power enters `NEUTRON-BLK-003` through a thick-target integral
  over the beam path length. The integral is sensitive to the low-energy end
  of the stopping curve (deuterons below 200 keV) where SRIM and ICRU 49
  diverge more than at high energy (shell corrections dominate below ~500
  keV/u). This is the highest-risk energy range for the source-equivalence
  claim.

## 6. Decision list — what would need to be true for acceptance

1. **Dense-plasma correction scope declared.** The reviewer must explicitly
   declare whether the beam-target stopping power for `NEUTRON-BLK-003`
   is computed in cold-matter approximation (DPF pinch treated as cold D₂
   gas at fill density) or with a plasma stopping correction. If cold-matter
   approximation, this must be flagged as an approximation in all downstream
   runtime claims with an explicit uncertainty bound (~10–30% on stopping,
   compounded into yield uncertainty).
2. **Three-way numerical cross-check completed.** SRIM, NIST PSTAR, and the
   IAEA calculator must all be run for deuterons on D₂ gas at the same
   pressure and energy range (0.1–10 MeV) and the results tabulated. If the
   three tools agree within 5%, the cold-matter approximation is internally
   consistent and any one can serve as the substitute target. If they
   disagree > 5% in the DPF beam-energy range, the discrepancy source must
   be identified before selection.
3. **Low-energy (< 500 keV) stopping confirmed.** The substitution is
   specifically vulnerable at the low-energy end of the deuteron beam.
   The chosen substitute must reproduce the shell-correction behaviour in
   the 100–500 keV deuteron range to within ICRU 49's stated uncertainty
   (5–8%). NIST PSTAR is preferred at this energy range because it uses
   the ICRU 49 I-values directly; SRIM should be cross-checked against
   PSTAR here explicitly.
4. **ICRU 49 secondary check completed.** Even if a free substitute is used
   as the primary extraction vehicle, a partial table from ICRU 49 (at least
   the D-on-H₂ entries, 1–10 MeV range, Table A.6 or equivalent) should be
   obtained via institutional access or ILL request to confirm the substitute
   is within acceptable error bounds.
5. **KR target extraction completed.** The chosen tool's output (a stopping-
   power table as a function of deuteron energy) must be archived as a
   SHA-256-verified file and ingested as a KR record.
