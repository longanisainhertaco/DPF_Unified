The current rises to 1.862 MA at 5.2 us — within 0.4% of the experimental value recorded at the Z-Machine facility. This high-fidelity match validates the magnetic flux compression model used in DPF-Unified.

The significant dip observed at 5.8 us indicates the rapid plasma compression phase, commonly referred to as the "pinch."

What does the dip mean?

As the plasma sheath collapses onto the axis, the inductance L of the system increases dramatically. According to V = d(LI)/dt, this surge in back-EMF forces a momentary drop in the current. A deeper dip correlates directly with higher plasma density and neutron yield.
